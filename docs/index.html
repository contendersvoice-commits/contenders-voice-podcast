Book of Genesis

VaYishlach (“He sent”)

Are You Authentic ?

Part 1

Last week, in parsha VaYetzei, we read about Jacob leaving his comfortable home and familiar surroundings in Beersheba to go forth to Padan-aram in Mesopotamia, and visit his parents’ relatives. In doing so, Jacob was taking his first steps toward fulfilling his destiny and becoming spiritually transformed in the process.

Real followers pass the test.

Central truth: The Father loves us too much to allow us to remain in the same spiritual place and condition for long, or settle back in familiar but unprofitable comfort zones. He wants us to continue to grow stronger in faith and move unto deeper and more intimate fellowship with Him. Our Messiah spoke to His disciples in this way: “If you abide [ obey, endure, stay in place] in my word, you are truly my disciples.” Understand: What this phrase, “truly my disciples” implies is that there are disciples who are not truly disciples. The word “truly” means “really”--as in ”really my disciples.” In any case, it has been said that a life without duty, discipline, or commitment, i.e. purpose, is bereft of satisfaction and joy. Do you agree?

Created for good works.

In other words there are real and unreal disciples who do not conform to or care about the teaching of Yeshua. There are both authentic and inauthentic disciples in the Church. The imposters are not led by the Holy Spirit. Consider: There is discipleship that is merely outward (a big show), and a discipleship that is faith-filled, inspirational, and completely sincere. So, to which group do you really belong, dear reader. “If you abide [obey] in My Word” is the test. Genuine disciples have eternity in their vision and are positioned for victory and blessing as they grow into overcomers and mature as the Bride of Messiah. See Revelation 2 and 3.

Steps to Discipleship.

This week, in parsha VaYishlach, we read the record of Jacob’s flight from Padan-aram back to Beersheba. However, it appears that his eventful return did not go quite as he had envisioned. The report of his troubled trek back to Canaan could be given this way: “A man’s heart devises the way; but the LORD directs his steps” (Proverbs 16:9). This is very good news for all attentive believers, wouldn’t you say?

The reminder applies to the Christian believers of this age, and also to the descendants of the scattered tribes of the House of Israel who are endeavoring to return to and settle into their Hebraic, Torah-based heritage. This is the birthright blessing attained by believers through their faith in Messiah Yeshua as Savior and sole Mediator of the New Covenant. Hebrews 8:6;12:24. But be informed. Modern saints have been called to contend for [recover and restore] the Torah and Spirit rooted faith that was once delivered to the apostolic, New Covenant Judean Ecclesia (Kahal). Over the centuries however, this faith been compromised, vitiated, and broken apart by false teachings and wandering religious orthodoxies. Determination and endurance are the pre-requisites for recovering the original New Testament apostolic faith and abiding in its truth. This presents a duty to modern disciples.

Parsha VaYishlach (“He sent’)

References: Genesis 32:3-36:43; Hosea 11:7-12:12; Joshua 11:5-19; First Corinthians 5:1-13; Revelation 7:1-12.

Apply the features of Jacob’s journey to your own.

We cannot cover in detail the complete narrative of this rich parsha in our “Points” briefing. However, we will glean relevant lessons from the narrative of Jacob’s journey home to Beersheba. Dear reader, you will be inspired by witnessing his climatic spiritual transformation which occurred during his eventful trek homeward. Goal: Start abiding in the light of divine truth known to you just as the patriarch did when he set out on his arduous journey. You will learn that no matter what resistance or trials you encounter, as you honor Yahweh you will be become transformed as well, but into your Savior’s likeness. Such is a wonderful opportunity, and privilege! Hence it is written: “Trust in YHWH with all thine heart and lean not on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge Him and He shall direct your paths” (Proverbs 3:5-6). So, let’s consider the following six points.

Providential Oversight.

  1. 1. “...return to the land of your fathers..“ (Gen, 31:3). After twenty years of serving his father-in-law Laban in Padam-aram, Yahweh directed Jacob to return to Canaan, the land of his father Abraham. And Jacob went, taking his family and flocks with him, and Yahweh’s reassuring promises, such as: ”I will be with you.” Such an affirmation would be hugely encouraging to any traveler. We have ours too, as we walk the narrow spoken of by Yeshua! Remember Matthew 28:16-20 and Joshua 1:9. Notwithstanding, Jacob left Padan-aram but shortly afterwards was confronted by Laban who pursued him. Their issues were settled with a covenant. Learn: Trust in your Father’s faithfulness and compassionate care, friend, concerning any confrontation or circumstance that suddenly comes upon you.

A heavenly escort. How good is that?

2. “...the angels of God met him“ (32:1). Jacob continued southward to the Jabbok River where heavenly angels, the messengers of Almighty God, intercepted him [fell in with him]. He was informed by his new escort service that Esau was nearby, and to be prepared. Note: This encounter was not just a vision, it was a supernatural manifestation of heavenly ministers. Jacob recognized them as God’s host–or Mananeh. Learn: A heavenly escort is an emblem of angelic guardianship which “encampeth round about them that fear [reverence and trust] the LORD and delivereth them…” and “..keepeth them in all their ways” (Psalms 34 and 91). That includes you, dear reader. You are highly valued and your faith journey is being chaperoned by a heaavenly escort, even now!

Be courageous and strong. Be not dismayed or afraid.

3. “Then the messengers returned...“ (32:6). Having learned of Esau’s approach, Jacob “...sent messengers before him to his brother Esau in the land of Seir, the country of Edom’” (B’resheit 32:3). They returned to inform the weary traveler that his brother had 400 armed men with him. Jacob feared violent consequences could occur during this “reunion” so he acted quickly and wisely. The dutiful patriarch divided his family and flocks into two camps behind him. And he prayed, “Oh God of my father Abraham and God of my father Isaac, Oh LORD…deliver me I pray thee”. Jacob prayed with humility and intensity unto YHWH who—as pointed out already, had made wonderful promises to his fathers of which he was the heir. Learn: He says to you personally: “The LORD your God is with you. He is mighty to save. He will take great delight in you, He will quiet you with His love, He will rejoice over you with singing” (Zephaniah 3:17). Reader, hold to your hope.

Give gifts. It Works.

4. “...so he took what came to his hand…” (32:13). Jacob believed the threat he perceived could not actually be reconciled. It appeared to him that the looming rapproachment [the restoration of happy relations] was beyond his ability to handle. So he sought the one who said, “I will surely do good and make your seed as the sand of the sea.” Jacob was given wisdom, the most valuable tool for handling confrontations—i. e., spiritual warfare. Hence, sent a series of gifts–herds and flocks–to soften the temperament of Esau and show respect to his older brother with courteous words, such as referring to him as “my master.” As a result, their meeting was amiable, and after friendly exchanges and assurances they separated. Then Jacob sent his family over the Jabbok tributary but he remained alone. Learn: Kindness and courtesy honor Yahweh and work wonders with your fellow man.

Soft words break bones–stubbornness.

5. “In this manner you shall speak to him…” (32:19). Jacob grew in wisdom and his experience had given him an acute understanding of human nature. He learned that kindness and warm words could soften a hard heart. “I will appease him with a present.” Learn: A repetition of generous presents would act upon Esau in the same way a blacksmith’s hammer, through repeated strikes, shapes the posture of hot iron. There would be a notable effect on the brothers’ reunion by way of Jacob’s wise action–-the fruit of an affectionate reunion. Learn: The Holy One of Israel acknowledges and blesses a generous giver. Remember the poor widow and her two-penny offering? She’s in the Bible’s Hall of Fame for eternity, friend, and rich beyond calculation.

Engage in unrelenting prayer.

6. “Then Jacob was left alone, and a man wrestled with him…” ( 32:22-32). In the verse we witness the mysterious wrestling match between Jacob and a man we interpret to be the visible, physical representation of the invisible Elohim. The confrontation occurred abruptly at a time when Jacob was engrossed in devout, heartfelt prayer. The contest was indeed a physical effort, but foremost it was an intense struggle preoccupying the soul of Jacob. He wanted and needed an enduring blessing, as do many of us today. However, he was given first a dislocated hip, of all thing! Yet Jacob persisted in striving with his heavenly Opponent. Point: Perhaps the dislocated hip was a sign and reminder that Jacob would never succeed in fulfilling his destiny by depending upon his own human strength and carnal temperament. Rather, such would be accomplished only by full reliance on the Almighty. Period. The Contender, allowed Jacob to prevail, and graciously gave him a blessed new name, Israel. It means “one who prevails with God and man.” Learn: Emulate Jacob. Here’s your verse. Live it! “Let us not grow weary in doing good, for in due season we will reap if we don’t give up” (Gal. 6:9). Keep Persevering, blessed reader.

Conclusion of VaYishlach, Part 1

The featured confrontation took place on the north side of the Jabbok river at night where Jacob had been alone, communing in his heart and making supplication and confession. It was at that time of focused prayer the heavenly Visitor suddenly approached him and the physical struggle began. Learn: 1. Set aside an altar time of reading and prayer every day. 2. Increase your attention span. 3. Start listening for His voice. 5. Abide in His Word. Point: Your Elohim, who hears, is bigger than your troubles and stronger than your enemies. He will bless you, reader, according to your need. Therefore, keep seeking his face. Trust His promises. Deepen your relationship with Him, Shaddai–Almighty God—the All Sufficient One. And especially be baptized in the Holy Spirit and Fire. and you will do well. Amen.

A life-changing encounter for Jacob.

At night, on his return journey home, Jacob came to the River Jabbok. He sent his large

family across the river and was afterwards left alone. Then, as it is written: “... a man

wrestled with him until the breaking of the day.” How the contest started we are not informed. Nevertheless, when the man saw that he did not prevail against Jacob, he put his hip out of socket as they wrestled. Then he said, “Let me go because the day has broken.” But Jacob said, “I will not let you go until you bless me.” And he said, “What is your name?” And the young sojourner said, “Jacob.” Then he said, “Your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but Israel, for you have striven with God and man and have prevailed.” Genesis 34:24-28. Note: There are several standards to point out that contributed to Jacob’s transformation into a new man, “Israel.” And these points could inspire you as well, reader, depending on your spiritual ambition of course.

1.The reluctance to release his opponent, even when his thigh joint had been dislocated, indicates Jacob’s unwavering determination to be blessed by God in a meaningful and most needed way. Point: Despite circumstances, a modern saint must also persevere in fervent prayer before the Throne of Grace. The believer is instructed to pray always with “all prayer and supplication in the Spirit” (Ephesians 6:18).

2. Jacob was given a new name, Israel. It makes a statement that Jacob was one who would strive with the Almighty until he prevailed. Point: Friend, do likewise! Set your course to excel as an overcomer. Strive with Yahweh to be blessed in this worthy campaign and you will also receive a new name from Yeshua, your High Priest. Revelation 2:17!

3. Jacob was spiritually elevated by the blessing bestowed upon him and came to understand the importance of God’s favor and his need for providential care. Point: Seek to honor Him in all things. Learn to be led and instructed by the Holy Spirit.. Wisdom is a weapon of warfare. Ephesians 1:18!

4. The circumstances of his previous years had changed Jacob, yet the Jabbok River event positioned Jacob in a higher spiritual level altogether. Jacob embraced the true God and His precepts as did His father Isaac, and grandfather, Abraham. [Their instruction likely came from Shem who was alive at that time.] Point: Jacob learned obedience. New Covenant saint, grow up and learn obedience too. There’s no room for hypocrisy in your quest to rank among the blessed overcomers. You may also have divine, life-changing divine encounters with Elohim, your Creator. Expect to hear His voice by the Spirit and encounter His presence in a discernable way. Get baptized in the Holy Spirit. See: Acts 1:1-9 and 2.1-47; 10:44-46; 19:6; 1 Cor. 14.

Follow Yahweh’s truth, not the endless banter humans.

Obedience is not legalism. It is the pure, biblical demonstration of faith and love for our Creator and Redeemer. You and I can spend months – pursuing and engaging political conflicts, advancing technology, assorted ideologies, financial upheavals and their threats, war, and religious inventions and controversies, to cite a few common preoccupations. But all that stuff will, in time, become repetitive and wearisome. Point: As Solomon realized as he drew close to his death, man’s chief duty is to obey God’s sacred Word. Our battle’s core is spiritual–always. If we won’t follow our Master after “His manner,” we will continue to abide under a demonic curse. Selah. 2 Chronicles 7:14 waits to be fiulfilled. “If my people, who are called…” You know the rest. But did you know that without obedience to known, applicable, and doable Scriptural truth there is no real repentance and no true discipleship? Pray. Wait upon the Spirit of Truth to come alongside you. And remember the plight of King Saul.

Peniel:

Did you know that locations can leave an impression on people that causes changes in their world view, stirs introspection, and even inspires personal development. Thus the Almighty chooses appropriate places for His work with people. Jacob named the place of his encounter with the heavenly wrestler, Peniel. The generally accepted meaning of the name is: For I have seen God face to face. However, since no human can see Elohim’s face and live, the name could refer to a close, spiritually intimate interaction. Keep in mind, friend. You can do your own research and decide for yourself concerning anything written in our blogs. This has always been our counsel concerning what Contenders Voice ministries happen to teach.

Note: Following this amazing visitation, an existential work of grace, Jacob went forward fully prepared to meet and be reconciled with his once-hostile brother, Esau.

The Torah is the standard of Discipleship

The study of the Torah is essential for Christians, and everyone else, for that matter. The Torah’s origin is divine, it is not restricted, but has a universal application. “It has been evident that believers, including preachers, often interpret the Bible through the lens of their culture, worldview, and public education. Unabridged, expository proclamations are what is needed now. VaYetzei’s related three closing points should find applications in your own lives, my persistent friends.

He will visit you.

Point 1. “Jacob departed…and encountered a place and spent the night there…” (10-11). The location of Jacob’s encounter with Yeshua was Mount Moriah, the site where Abraham bound Isaac on the altar and where the Temple would later stand. Point: For disciples to desire a visitation of the Holy One of Israel is biblical. Learn: Jacob is a type of each one of us who is awakening and coming to our biblical heritage. Moses pleaded :“Please show me your glory.” Yahweh responds: “I will do this very thing you have asked…” (Exodus 33:18). Moses had found favor in God’s sight. WHY? What’s your answer?

You are sent also. What is your purpose?
